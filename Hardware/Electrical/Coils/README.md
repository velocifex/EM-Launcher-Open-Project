The electromagnet coil's parameters of inductance and resistance, determined by the wire diameter and number of turns and layers, are among the most important values to design in a coilgun! They, along with the capacitor's voltage and capacitance, determine how much force will be applied to the projectile. This project has gotten some progress in optimizing the coils for high projectile velocity and energy, but there is still plenty of experimentation that can be done.

The most recent prototype uses coils of roughly the following specifications:
- 20mm in length (along the barrel), defined by the bobbin it's wound around.
- The projectile is 0.125" by 1.5" in cross-section, the inside of the coil bobbin is slightly larger (<1mm) for clearance. The thin wall of the bobbin that the coil winds around adds about 4mm more to the total coil internal width and height. So the coil's inner rectangular cross-sectional dimensions would be roughly 8mm by 43mm.
- Wire is 22AWG copper enameled wire, also called "magnet wire". It is wound as tightly as possible with few or no gaps between turns.
- Each coil was wound with enough "layers" of turns to give it about 500 microhenries of inductance and 1 ohm of resistance.
	- There were some experiments with using higher inductance and resistance coils towards the breech, and lower inductance and resistance towards the muzzle. This may help performance, and is a subject of further experimentation.

The coils should be connected to power in such a way that they all orient their magnetic fields in the same direction within the barrel. In other words, their magnetic polarities are aligned. This helps efficiency because, when one coil stage turns off and the next coil turns on, the later stage can sort of "move" the previous one's existing magnetic field towards itself, rather than having to make a whole new magnetic field in the barrel "from scratch". Accordingly, it helps the later coil's current rise and the earlier coil's current fall faster, which is essential to efficiently moving the magnetic field ahead of the projectile.
To facilitate proper connection, each coil has a yellow and purple wire soldered to each end, which should be connected to the positive and negative power busbars (through the power switch) respectively. The arbitrary convention in this project is to have the coils' magnetic south pole point towards the muzzle and the north pole towards the breech. The yellow and purple wires were soldered to the appropriate ends of the coil such that the current flow would create this magnetic field arrangement.
